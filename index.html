Case study

HookahPOS

A complete point-of-sale system for hookah lounges: floor plan, orders, payments, inventory, staff and analytics. It is a commercial product. A new installation starts empty, and the venue owner sets up rooms, menu, stock and staff themselves.

The problem

Generic restaurant POS software doesn't fit how a hookah lounge works. It has no concept of hookah masters, tobacco recipes, coal changes, or tables that stay open for hours and get split between friends at the end.

Staff work on a 1920×1080 touch terminal during a busy evening shift, so every action has to take one or two taps. Managers check the numbers from their phones.

What I built

  • Floor plan with a layout editor, live table status and reservations
  • Orders: hookah master assignment, comments on a built-in touch keyboard, sending to the bar
  • Payments: card and cash, discounts, voids, splitting one bill into several
  • Inventory: menu recipes write off stock automatically, with a movement log
  • Staff with PIN login, roles, shifts and worked hours
  • Analytics by business day, month and year; top items; discounts and voids
  • Installer: a self-contained Windows package that runs on a single venue PC

Engineering

Decisions that matter

A POS system handles money and stock, so mistakes are expensive. These are the rules the system is built around.

01Orders are snapshots

Each order line stores the item's name, price and recipe as they were at the moment of sale (recipe_snapshot). Editing the menu tomorrow never changes a bill that has already been paid.

02Stock changes in one transaction

Ingredient quantities change only through StockService. The new balance and the movement log entry are written in one database transaction, so stock and its history can't drift apart.

03Partial actions split the line

A discount, a void or moving "1 of 3 mojitos" to another bill splits the order line first. Every line then has exactly one state, which keeps totals and reports correct.

04The server owns the money

All totals, discounts and splits are calculated on the server. The React client only displays them, so two terminals can never show different amounts for the same bill.

05"Today" is a business day

A lounge closes at 2 a.m., so a shift belongs to the day it started (cutoff configurable, 06:00 by default). All times are stored in UTC, and the switch to daylight saving time, when 02:00 doesn't exist, is handled explicitly.

06Instant session revocation

Staff log in with a PIN and receive a JWT. Every request checks a token version against the database, so changing a role or PIN, or dismissing an employee, logs them out on every device immediately.

07Nothing is physically deleted

Employees, menu items, tables and rooms are archived, not deleted. Reports for past months stay complete, and schema migrations move existing data instead of dropping it.

08Translate the system, not the data

The interface and server messages come in Polish, English and Russian, chosen by Accept-Language. The menu stays exactly as the owner typed it, because it is printed on the Polish fiscal receipt.
